🏛️ 来源历史:
available 出现于晚期中古英语，由 avail 与 -able 构成，早期含有“有效用、有帮助”的意思。avail 的词源与古法语 valoir“有价值”和拉丁语 valere“强健、有力量”有关，available 后来发展出“可供使用、可以获得”的常用义。
Available appeared in late Middle English from avail and -able, initially conveying usefulness or effectiveness. Avail is historically connected with Old French valoir, meaning 'to be worth,' and Latin valere, meaning 'to be strong'; available later developed its familiar meanings of being obtainable or ready for use.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代构词联系：avail + -able → available；available + -ity → availability（拼写变为 -ability）。常用词族：avail（有帮助；利用，常见于 avail oneself of）、available（可获得的）、availability（可获得性；空闲情况）、unavailable（无法获得的；没空的）、unavailability（无法获得；无空闲）；结构本身不表示“免费”。
Modern morphological connections: avail + -able → available; available + -ity → availability, with the ending changing to -ability. The common family includes avail ('be useful' or 'make use of,' especially in avail oneself of), available, availability, unavailable, and unavailability; the structure does not imply that something is free of charge.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
accessible（可接触或获取的）侧重能否接近、进入或取得；obtainable（可获得的）侧重能否设法取得；free（空闲的；未被占用的）可替换表示人有空或位置空余的 available，但 free 还可能表示“免费”，available 本身没有这一含义。
Accessible emphasizes being reachable or accessible for use; obtainable emphasizes the possibility of acquiring something; free overlaps with available when a person is unoccupied or a place is unused, but free can also mean 'without payment,' which available does not imply.
💡 秒记锚点:
抓住“可供你用”：东西拿得到、人有空、座位未占用；可用不等于免费。
Think 'ready for you': an item you can obtain, a person with time, or a seat you can take; availability does not imply zero cost.

🏛️ 来源历史:
borrow 经中古英语 borwen 源自古英语 borgian，与表示“担保、抵押”的 borg 有历史联系。它的核心意义一直围绕借入并承担归还义务，后来也用于借鉴语言、思想或风格。
Borrow comes through Middle English borwen from Old English borgian, historically related to borg, meaning a pledge or security. Its central meaning concerns receiving something with an obligation to return or repay it, and it later extended to adopting words, ideas, and styles.
🧩 构词衍生:
borrow 是现代英语中的完整词基，不能拆成 bor + row。常用词族：borrow + -er → borrower（借用者；借款人）；borrow + -ing → borrowing（借用；借款；借入的词语或元素）；borrow + -ed → borrowed（借来的；借用的），另有复数 borrowings（多笔借款；借入成分）。
Borrow is a complete base in modern English and is not meaningfully divided into bor + row. Its common family includes borrow + -er → borrower, borrow + -ing → borrowing ('the act of borrowing,' 'borrowed funds,' or 'an adopted word or feature'), and borrow + -ed → borrowed; borrowings can refer to multiple loans or adopted elements.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
take out（办理、取得）常用于 take out a loan，强调取得贷款；adopt（采用）与借鉴义接近，但不强调来源像 borrow 那样明确；lend（借出）是方向相反的对应词：borrow 是从别人那里借入，lend 是把自己的东西借给别人。
Take out overlaps with financial borrow in take out a loan and emphasizes obtaining a loan; adopt overlaps with borrowing ideas but need not highlight their source. Lend is the directional counterpart rather than a synonym: you borrow something from someone, while they lend it to you.
💡 秒记锚点:
借入方向朝自己：borrow from；借出方向朝别人：lend to。借物要还物，借钱要还款，借思想则是采用。
Track the direction: borrow from brings something to you; lend to sends it to someone else. Objects are returned, money is repaid, and ideas are adopted.

Budget comes from Middle English bowgette, borrowed from French bougette, meaning a small leather bag. Through its association with a bag containing financial papers and the British Chancellor's financial proposals, it developed the meaning of a financial plan, followed by the verb and the adjective meaning inexpensive.

Budget is a complete base, not a meaningful combination of bud + get. Spending plan is close to the planning sense, although a budget may also include income; allocate overlaps with the verb but emphasizes assigning resources to particular purposes; inexpensive overlaps with the adjective, while budget often identifies a low-cost category of product or service.
💡 秒记锚点:
从“小钱袋”记起：袋里能放多少、钱怎么分配，就是预算；为小钱袋设计的服务，就是经济型服务。
Picture the original small money bag: deciding what goes into it and how to divide its contents gives a budget; services designed for a small bag of money are budget services.

🏛️ 来源历史:
commute 源自拉丁语 commutare“改变、交换”，由 com- 与 mutare“改变”构成，英语早期也有替换、转换之义。美国铁路的 commutation ticket 指以优惠套票替代逐次购票的车票，commute 的现代通勤义由这种经常往返的乘车方式发展而来。
Commute comes from Latin commutare, meaning 'to change or exchange,' formed from com- and mutare, 'to change'; early English uses likewise involved substitution or conversion. Its travel sense developed through American railway commutation tickets, which replaced separate fares with a reduced rate for repeated journeys.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代以 commute 为完整词基，不按 com + mute“静音”拆义。commute 去掉词尾不发音的 e，再加 -er 或 -ing，得到 commuter（通勤者）和 commuting（通勤）；常用及相关词族还包括 commuted（通勤了；被减轻的）、commutable（可通勤到达的；可转换或减轻的）、commutation（减刑；折算；交换），数学和技术相关词有 commutative（可交换的）、commutativity（交换性）和 commutator（换向器；交换子）。
Use commute as the complete modern base rather than interpreting it as com + mute meaning 'silent.' Drop the final silent e before -er or -ing to form commuter and commuting; the common and related family also includes commuted, commutable ('reachable by commuting' or 'capable of conversion or reduction'), and commutation ('sentence reduction,' 'conversion,' or 'exchange'), with commutative, commutativity, and commutator in mathematics or technology.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
travel（出行）范围更广，不要求固定往返；shuttle（往返穿梭）强调两地间反复移动，不一定是上下班；reduce（减轻）可描述降低刑罚程度，而 commute 特指由有关当局正式以较轻刑罚替换原刑罚。
Travel is broader and does not require a regular return journey; shuttle emphasizes repeated movement between places without necessarily involving work; reduce can describe making a punishment lighter, while commute specifically means officially substituting a less severe punishment.
💡 秒记锚点:
历史核心是“转换”：刑罚换轻；通勤义则由反复乘车的优惠套票发展而来。
Anchor it in 'exchange': a punishment is exchanged for a lighter one, while commuting grew from tickets for repeated journeys.

Deposit ultimately comes from Latin deponere, 'to put down or entrust,' formed from de-, 'down,' and ponere, 'to put'; its past participle was depositus, and depositum referred to something entrusted for safekeeping. The idea of placing or entrusting something developed into the English senses involving bank funds, security payments, and accumulated material; repayment conditions depend on the agreement.

In modern use, deposit is normally treated as a complete base; the historical elements de- and ponere/posit- help explain 'putting down' but do not determine the conditions attached to a payment. Its common family includes deposits (a plural noun or third-person singular verb), deposited, depositing, depositor, and depository; related Latinate formations include deposition ('sediment accumulation,' 'the taking of testimony,' or 'removal from office'), depositional, and the more formal depositary ('an entrusted custodian or institution').
⚖️ 近义微雕:
down payment（首付款）通常是总价的一部分，不用于泛指所有押金；security（担保金）强调对履约、损失或损坏的保障；sediment（沉积物）指沉降形成的物质，而 deposit 还可指矿床、表面附着层或其他积聚物。
Down payment usually means an initial part of a purchase price and does not cover every security deposit; security emphasizes protection against nonperformance, loss, or damage; sediment is material formed by settling, whereas deposit also covers mineral accumulations, surface layers, and other collected substances.
💡 秒记锚点:
统一记“放到某处”：钱放银行是存款，钱交对方保管是押金，提前放一部分价款是订金，泥沙留下来是沉积。
Anchor every sense in 'putting something down': money in a bank, security held by someone, part of a price paid ahead, or sediment left in a place.

Modern structure: land + lord → landlord, a lexicalized compound noun; a landlord need not be an aristocrat or let land specifically. Its family includes landlords, landlordism (the system or practices of landlords), and landlordship (the status or position of a landlord); the corresponding female term is landlady, plural landladies.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
property owner（物业所有者）强调所有权，不一定出租；lessor（出租方）是合同中的正式称谓；publican（酒馆经营者）对应英国英语中的酒馆老板义，不指一般房东。
A property owner owns property but does not necessarily rent it out; lessor is the formal contractual term for the party granting a lease; publican matches the British pub sense rather than the general property sense.
💡 秒记锚点:
land + lord：物业的“主人”把使用权租给你，就是房东；lord 在这里不要求贵族身份。
Land + lord: the property's 'master' rents you the right to use it; no aristocratic title is required.
