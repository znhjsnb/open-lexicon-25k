COBUILD: A biscuit is a small, flat cake that is usually crisp and sweet, although some types are savoury.
搭配: chocolate biscuits; a packet of biscuits 巧克力饼干；一包饼干

COBUILD: In American English, a biscuit is a small, soft bread made without yeast and often served warm with a meal.

🏛️ 来源历史:
经古法语 bescuit 传入英语，最终源于拉丁语 bis“两次”和 coctus“烤熟的”，原指经过两次烘烤、便于保存的干硬面饼。后来英式英语用它指饼干，美式英语则发展出松软小圆面包这一含义。
Biscuit entered English through Old French bescuit, ultimately from Latin bis, meaning 'twice', and coctus, meaning 'cooked', referring to hard bread baked twice for preservation. British English developed the crisp-snack meaning, while American English also developed the soft-quick-bread meaning.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中 biscuit 通常作为完整词根使用，不按 bis + cuit 进行能产构词；复数为 biscuits。主要衍生词和复合词：biscuity（像饼干的；有饼干味的）、biscuit-coloured／biscuit-colored（饼干色的）、biscuit tin（饼干盒）、biscuit cutter（饼干或美式小圆面包切模）；bis“两次”与 coctus“烤熟的”属于历史词源分析。
In modern English, biscuit normally functions as a whole base rather than a productive combination of bis and cuit; its plural is biscuits. Principal derivatives and compounds include biscuity, biscuit-coloured/biscuit-colored, biscuit tin, and biscuit cutter; bis 'twice' and coctus 'cooked' belong to its historical analysis.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
cookie（曲奇；饼干）是美式英语中甜饼干的常用名称，英式英语中常指较厚、带巧克力豆等配料的饼干；cracker（薄脆饼干）通常较薄、较脆且偏咸；scone（司康饼）与美式 biscuit 相近，但常更甜，具体做法因地区而异。
Cookie is the usual American term for a sweet biscuit and often suggests a thicker biscuit with additions such as chocolate chips in British English; cracker usually means a thin, crisp, savoury biscuit; scone resembles an American biscuit but is often sweeter, with recipes varying by region.
💡 秒记锚点:
历史核心是“两次烤”：烤得干脆便于保存，记住英式饼干；另记美式 biscuit 是松软小面包。
Anchor the history in 'twice baked': dry and crisp for storage gives you the British biscuit; separately remember that an American biscuit is soft bread.

COBUILD: An oven is a piece of cooking equipment with an enclosed space in which food is cooked using heat.
搭配: preheat the oven; bake in the oven 预热烤箱；放进烤箱烘烤
例句: Preheat the oven to 180°C before you put the cake in.
放入蛋糕前，先把烤箱预热到180摄氏度。
🏛️ 来源历史:
来自古英语 ofen，与德语 Ofen“炉；烤箱”等日耳曼语词同源。它一直指利用封闭空间蓄热、烘烤食物的炉具，后来也用于现代家用烤箱。
Oven comes from Old English ofen and is related to German Ofen and other Germanic words for a heated chamber. Its central meaning has remained an enclosed space for baking, extending naturally to modern domestic appliances.
🧩 构词衍生:
oven 是现代英语中的单纯词，复数为 ovens。主要复合词：ovenproof（耐烤箱高温的）、oven-safe（可安全放入烤箱的）、oven-ready（已备妥、可直接入炉的）、oven-baked（烤箱烤制的）、ovenware（烤箱用器皿）、oven glove／oven mitt（隔热烤箱手套）；ovenproof 中的 -proof 表示“能承受……的”。
Oven is a simple modern English base, with the plural ovens. Principal compounds include ovenproof, oven-safe, oven-ready, oven-baked, ovenware, and oven glove/oven mitt; -proof in ovenproof means 'able to withstand'.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
stove（炉灶）侧重提供烹饪热源的设备，在美式英语中也可指包含烤箱的整套炉具；cooker（炊具；炉灶）在英式英语中常指带灶台和烤箱的整机；kiln（窑）主要用于烧制陶器、砖等，而不是日常烹饪。
Stove emphasizes cooking equipment that supplies heat and, in American English, can include an oven; cooker commonly means the complete cooking appliance in British English; kiln is mainly used for firing pottery or bricks rather than everyday cooking.
💡 秒记锚点:
抓住“封闭热腔”：食物放进去，靠周围的热量慢慢烤熟。
Think 'enclosed heat chamber': food goes inside and cooks in the surrounding heat.
fortnight 1 词性 · 双语对齐
高考CET4考研IELTSCEFR-B1
n. 可数名词 /英 /ˈfɔːtnaɪt/；美 /ˈfɔːrtnaɪt// （主要用于英式英语）两周；十四天 (a period of two weeks)
COBUILD: A fortnight is a period of fourteen days, or two weeks.
搭配: a fortnight ago; in a fortnight 两周前；两周后
例句: We're going away for a fortnight in July.
我们七月份要外出度假两周。
🏛️ 来源历史:
由古英语 fēowertīene niht“十四个夜晚”逐渐缩合而来，反映了过去用夜晚计算时间段的习惯。因此 fortnight 就是十四天，词首 fort- 与 fort“堡垒”无关。
Fortnight developed through contraction of Old English fēowertīene niht, meaning 'fourteen nights', reflecting an older practice of counting periods by nights. It therefore means fourteen days, and its opening fort- is unrelated to fort meaning a stronghold.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语通常将 fortnight 视为完整词根，复数为 fortnights；它在历史上由“十四＋夜晚”缩合而成。fortnight + -ly → fortnightly，可作形容词“每两周一次的”和副词“每两周一次”，也可作可数名词表示“双周刊”，复数为 fortnightlies。
Modern English generally treats fortnight as a whole base, with the plural fortnights; historically, it is a contracted combination of 'fourteen' and 'night'. Fortnight + -ly forms fortnightly, an adjective and adverb meaning 'every two weeks', and also a countable noun meaning a publication issued every two weeks, with the plural fortnightlies.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
two weeks（两周）在各类英语中都通用，也是美式英语更常见的说法；fourteen days（十四天）更突出确切天数；a couple of weeks（两个星期左右）在口语中可能表示大约两周，而 fortnight 通常明确表示两周。
Two weeks is universal and is the more usual American expression; fourteen days emphasizes the exact number of days; a couple of weeks can be approximate in conversation, whereas a fortnight normally specifies two weeks.
💡 秒记锚点:
fourteen nights → fortnight：十四个夜晚，就是两周。
Fourteen nights → fortnight: fourteen nights make two weeks.

COBUILD: Fur is the thick, soft hair that covers the bodies of animals such as cats, rabbits, and bears.
搭配: thick fur; soft fur 浓密的兽毛；柔软的兽毛
例句: The fox's thick fur keeps it warm in winter.
狐狸浓密的毛使它在冬天保持温暖。
n. 可数及不可数名词 /英 /fɜː/；美 /fɝː// 毛皮；裘皮；毛皮衣物 (animal skin with its hair attached, or clothing made from it)
COBUILD: Fur is an animal's skin with the hair still on it, used to make clothing; a fur or furs can also mean garments made from this material.
搭配: a fur coat; faux fur 一件裘皮大衣；人造毛皮
例句: The hood is trimmed with faux fur.
风帽的边缘饰有人造毛皮。
n. 不可数名词 /英 /fɜː/；美 /fɝː// （英式）水垢；壶或管道内壁的沉积物 (a hard mineral deposit inside a kettle or pipe)
COBUILD: In British English, fur is a hard layer of minerals that forms inside kettles or pipes, especially in areas with hard water.
搭配: a coating of fur; fur inside a kettle 一层水垢；水壶内部的水垢
例句: A thick coating of fur had formed inside the old kettle.
旧水壶的内壁已经结了一层厚厚的水垢。
v. 不及物及及物动词 /英 /fɜː/；美 /fɝː// （英式，常与 up 连用）结水垢；使结水垢 (to become coated with mineral deposits, or cause this to happen)
COBUILD: If a kettle or pipe furs up, or something furs it up, a layer of hard minerals forms inside it.
搭配: pipes fur up; fur up a kettle 管道结水垢；使水壶结水垢
例句: Hard water can fur up the pipes over time.
硬水久而久之会使管道内壁结上水垢。
v. 及物动词 /英 /fɜː/；美 /fɝː// 给……衬毛皮或饰毛皮 (to line, cover, or trim something with fur)
COBUILD: If a garment is furred, it is lined, covered, or trimmed with fur.
搭配: a furred hood; a furred robe 衬有或饰有毛皮的风帽；衬有或饰有毛皮的长袍
例句: The ceremonial robe was furred at the collar.
这件礼袍的领口饰有毛皮。
🏛️ 来源历史:
中古英语通过古法语中表示“给衣服加衬里”的词语吸收了这个词，早期含义与衣物衬里有关，后来转指带毛的兽皮和动物身上的毛。表示水垢等附着层的含义，是从“覆盖在表面的一层东西”引申而来。
Fur entered Middle English through Old French vocabulary for lining garments, with early meanings connected to clothing linings before developing the senses of hairy animal skins and animal hair. The deposit meaning arose by extension to a layer coating a surface.
🧩 构词衍生:
fur 是现代词根；表示兽毛或材料时通常不可数，表示毛皮衣物或种类时可用 furs。动词变化为 furs、furred、furring；fur + -y → furry（毛茸茸的），双写 r，比较级和最高级为 furrier、furriest。其他主要词族成员及复合词：furless（无毛的）、fur-like／furlike（像毛皮的）、furrier（皮货商；毛皮服装制作商，与 furry 的比较级同形）、furriery（毛皮业；毛皮制品）、fur-lined（衬毛皮的）、fur-bearing（长有可用毛皮的）。
Fur is a modern base; animal hair and the material are normally uncountable, while garments or types can be called furs. Verb forms are furs, furred, and furring; fur + -y gives furry, with r doubled, and its comparative and superlative are furrier and furriest. Other principal family members and compounds include furless, fur-like/furlike, furrier meaning a fur trader or garment maker, furriery, fur-lined, and fur-bearing; the occupational noun furrier has the same spelling as the comparative of furry.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
hair（毛；头发）是更广泛的词，可指人或动物的毛；pelt（带毛兽皮）侧重整张带毛的动物皮；fleece（羊毛；绒毛）通常指羊等动物的整层毛，也可指仿羊毛绒面料。
Hair is the broader term for strands growing on humans or animals; pelt emphasizes an animal's whole skin with its hair attached; fleece usually means the woolly coat of a sheep or similar animal and can also name a soft synthetic fabric.
💡 秒记锚点:
核心是一层覆盖物：动物身上是兽毛，衣服上是毛皮，水壶内壁则引申为水垢。
Think 'a covering layer': hair on an animal, a fur lining on clothing, and, by extension, mineral coating inside a kettle.

COBUILD: A drawer is a box-shaped part of a desk, cupboard, or other piece of furniture that you pull out to put things in or take things out.

COBUILD: Drawers are underpants or similar underwear worn over the lower part of the body; this is an old-fashioned use.
搭配: a pair of drawers; long drawers 一条内裤；长衬裤
例句: The museum displayed a pair of nineteenth-century cotton drawers.
博物馆展出了一条十九世纪的棉质衬裤。
🏛️ 来源历史:
由 draw“拉；画；开立票据”加名词后缀 -er 构成：家具中的 drawer 是可以拉出的箱格，指人时则是执行绘画或开票动作的人。复数 drawers 表示衬裤，与把衣物拉上身的动作有关。
Drawer combines draw with the noun-forming suffix -er: the furniture sense names a compartment that is pulled out, while the personal senses name someone who draws pictures or issues payment instruments. The plural drawers for underwear relates to pulling the garment on.
🧩 构词衍生:
draw + -er → drawer，-er 在这里可构成工具或事物名称，也可构成施事者名称；普通复数为 drawers，表示内裤时则固定用复数。基础词族：draw（原形）、draws（第三人称单数）、drew（过去式）、drawn（过去分词）、drawing（绘画；图画）、drawable（可绘制的）、drawee（付款人；受票人）；相关构词包括 redraw（重画）、withdraw（撤回；取款）、withdrawal（撤回；取款）、overdraw（透支）、drawerful（一抽屉的量）、top-drawer（一流的）。
Draw + -er forms drawer; here -er can name an object or instrument as well as a person performing an action. Its regular plural is drawers, and the underwear sense occurs in the plural. The base family includes draw, draws, drew, drawn, drawing, drawable, and drawee; related formations include redraw, withdraw, withdrawal, overdraw, drawerful, and top-drawer.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
compartment（隔间；分格）泛指分隔出来的空间，不一定能拉出；artist（艺术家；画家）强调艺术创作身份，比“画画的人”范围更广；issuer（签发人；发行人）泛指签发票据、证券等的人或机构，drawer 特指开立支票或汇票的一方。
Compartment is a general word for a divided storage space and does not imply that it slides out; artist emphasizes an artistic role and is broader than someone who draws; issuer can refer to anyone issuing documents or securities, while drawer specifically identifies the party drawing a cheque or bill of exchange.
💡 秒记锚点:
draw 是“拉”也是“画”：拉出来的格子是抽屉，画图的人是绘图者；draw a cheque 则是开支票。
Draw means both 'pull' and 'make a picture': a pull-out box is a drawer, and a person who draws is a drawer; drawing a cheque gives the financial sense.

COBUILD: An elephant is a very large animal with thick skin, large ears, and a long trunk that it uses to pick things up.
搭配: a herd of elephants; an elephant's trunk 一群象；象鼻
例句: The elephant used its trunk to pull leaves from a tree.

🏛️ 来源历史:
英语 elephant 经法语和拉丁语传入，最终可追溯到古希腊语 elephas，其属格为 elephantos，可指“象”或“象牙”。这个希腊语词更早的来源尚不确定。
Elephant entered English through French and Latin, ultimately from Greek elephas, with the genitive elephantos, meaning either an elephant or ivory. The Greek word's earlier origin is uncertain.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中 elephant 是不可再按常用词缀拆分的基本词。屈折变化：elephant + -s → elephants（复数）；主要派生词：elephantine（像象的；庞大笨重的）。相关医学词 elephantiasis（象皮病）含希腊语来源的 elephant- 和表示病理状态的 -iasis。
In modern English, elephant is a base word without a productive internal breakdown. Inflection: elephant + -s → elephants. The main derivative is elephantine, meaning elephant-like or huge and unwieldy. The related medical term elephantiasis contains Greek-derived elephant- and -iasis, a suffix denoting a pathological condition.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
pachyderm（厚皮兽）是较旧的宽泛称呼，也可包括犀牛等动物；tusker（长有大牙的动物，尤指雄象）突出象牙；jumbo（大象；庞然大物）是非正式称呼，也可指其他巨大事物。这些词都不能在所有语境中替换 elephant。
Pachyderm is an older, broader label that can also include animals such as rhinoceroses; tusker emphasizes prominent tusks, especially those of a male elephant; jumbo is an informal term for an elephant or another very large thing. None replaces elephant in every context.
💡 秒记锚点:
整词联想：长鼻、大耳、庞大身体 → elephant；elephantine 把“大象般庞大”的形象变成形容词。
Picture a trunk, large ears, and a huge body: elephant. Elephantine turns that image into the adjective 'huge and elephant-like.'

Nanny is recorded for a female caregiver in the eighteenth century and is generally associated with children's familiar names for adult women, although its precise origin is uncertain. The female-goat sense is associated with the personal nickname Nanny, also used as a pet form of Anne.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中 nanny 通常作为整词；名词直接转为动词，无需添加词缀。屈折变化：nanny → nannies（名词复数／动词第三人称单数）、nannied（过去式／过去分词）、nannying（现在分词／动名词）；辅音字母后的 y 在 -s、-ed 前变为 i，但在 -ing 前保留。常见组合：nanny goat（母山羊）、nanny state（保姆式国家，贬义）。
Nanny is normally treated as a whole base word; its verb is formed by conversion without an added affix. Inflections are nannies, nannied, and nannying. After a consonant, final y changes to i before -s or -ed, producing -ies or -ied, but remains before -ing. Common combinations include nanny goat and nanny state, a disapproving term for an excessively protective state.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
babysitter（临时照看孩子的人）通常只照看几个小时；childminder（托儿照看者，主要用于英式英语）通常在自己家中有偿照看孩子；au pair（互惠生）通常住在接待家庭，以照看孩子和少量家务换取食宿及零用钱。nanny 通常是较固定、持续受雇的育儿照料者。
A babysitter usually provides care for a few hours; a childminder, chiefly in British English, usually provides paid childcare in their own home; an au pair typically lives with a host family and provides childcare and light housework in exchange for accommodation and pocket money. A nanny usually has a more regular, ongoing childcare role.
💡 秒记锚点:
核心画面是“照看孩子的人”：nanny 作名词指照料者，作动词指照看；把成年人也当孩子照看，就引申为“过度管束”。
Start with someone caring for children: a nanny is the caregiver, and to nanny is to provide that care. Treat adults like children, and the verb becomes 'overprotect or overcontrol.'

Shiver, meaning 'tremble,' developed from Middle English chiveren, whose earlier origin is uncertain. Its use extends from bodily shaking caused by cold to trembling with fear, anticipation, or excitement, and the noun names such a reaction.

Rip in the sense 'tear' comes from Middle English rippen and may be related to a Middle Dutch word of the same form meaning 'tear or strip,' although the precise origin is uncertain. In computing, the idea of taking something out developed into extracting audio or video from a disc.

Rip is an indivisible modern base used as both a verb and a noun. Inflections are rips, ripped, and ripping; final p doubles before -ed and -ing because it follows a stressed short vowel. Family members and common combinations include ripper, ripped, rip-off, and ripcord. Ripped can mean torn or visibly muscular; a rip-off is an overcharge or inferior imitation. Common phrasal verbs include rip up, meaning tear into pieces; rip off, meaning remove by tearing, overcharge, or copy; and rip into, meaning criticize fiercely.
⚖️ 近义微雕:

Tear is the broadest term and need not emphasize force; rend is formal or literary and suggests violent tearing; split emphasizes separation into parts without necessarily involving a tearing action. Rip usually highlights quick, forceful tearing.

Cage entered English through Old French cage from Latin cavea, meaning a cage, enclosure, or enclosed space, related to cavus, 'hollow.' The noun later became a verb meaning 'put in a cage,' with figurative uses referring to restricted freedom.
🧩 构词衍生:

Cage is a modern base word, and the verb is formed from the noun by conversion without an added affix. Inflections are cages, caged, and caging; the final silent e is dropped before -ing. Main family members include caged, uncage, uncaged, and cageless. Birdcage is a common compound.
