The word developed from a French sausage name such as cervelas, related to Italian cervellata and ultimately cervello, meaning 'brain', reflecting an early ingredient. Modern saveloys are generally meat sausages, and the name does not imply that they still contain brain.

English borrowed the botanical Latin genus name Solidago, continuing an earlier Latin plant name associated with solidare, 'to make firm or whole'. The name reflects traditional use in treating wounds rather than establishing modern medical effectiveness.

Modern structure: solidago is a borrowed plant name, not English solid + go. Historically it can be associated with Latin solid-, meaning 'whole' or 'firm', and the plant-name formation -ago, but this is not a productive modern English pattern. The common-noun plural is solidagos; capitalized Solidago is the genus name, and goldenrod is the usual common name. There are no common derived English verbs or adverbs.
