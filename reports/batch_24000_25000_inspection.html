metacarpus 1 词性 · 双语对齐
n. 名词 //ˌmetəˈkɑːpəs// 掌部；腕部与手指之间、以五块掌骨为骨架的手部区域 (the region of the hand between the wrist and the fingers, supported by five metacarpal bones)
COBUILD: The metacarpus is the part of your hand between your wrist and your fingers, containing the five metacarpal bones.
搭配: bones of the metacarpus; fractures of the metacarpus 掌部的骨骼；掌部骨折
例句: The diagram shows the five bones of the metacarpus between the wrist and the fingers.
这幅图显示了腕部与手指之间的五块掌骨。
🏛️ 来源历史:
该词借自新拉丁语 metacarpus，由希腊语来源的 meta-（在……之后、越过）和 karpos（腕部）构成。它以解剖位置命名，指越过腕部、通向手指的掌部。
The word was borrowed from New Latin metacarpus, formed from Greek-derived meta-, meaning 'beyond' or 'after', and karpos, meaning 'wrist'. Its anatomical meaning identifies the region beyond the wrist toward the fingers.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代科学构词可分析为 meta-（越过）+ carp-（腕）+ -us（保留的拉丁语名词词尾）；普通英语中通常把全词作为专业名词学习。复数为 metacarpi；相关形式有 metacarpal（掌部的；掌骨）、metacarpals（多块掌骨）及 metacarpally（在掌部，罕见）。
In scientific morphology, the word can be analyzed as meta- ('beyond') + carp- ('wrist') + the retained Latin noun ending -us; in ordinary English it is usually learned as a technical whole. Its plural is metacarpi; related forms include metacarpal ('relating to the metacarpus'; 'a metacarpal bone'), metacarpals, and the rare adverb metacarpally.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
metacarpal region（掌部区域）是接近的解剖表达；palm（手掌）通常侧重手的掌侧表面；metacarpal bones（掌骨）只指骨骼，不包括整个掌部区域。
Metacarpal region is a close anatomical equivalent; palm usually emphasizes the inner surface of the hand; metacarpal bones refers specifically to the bones rather than the entire region.
💡 秒记锚点:
meta-＝越过，carp-＝腕：越过手腕、尚未到手指，就是掌部。
Meta- means 'beyond' and carp- means 'wrist': beyond the wrist, before the fingers, lies the metacarpus.

COBUILD: A mizenmast is a mast behind a sailing ship's mainmast, usually the last mast on a ship with three masts.
搭配: the foot of the mizenmast; the top of the mizenmast 后桅根部；后桅顶端
例句: A sailor climbed to the top of the mizenmast to inspect the rigging.

🏛️ 来源历史:
mizen 是 mizzen 的另一种拼写，其词源经法语追溯到意大利语 mezzana，最终与拉丁语表示“中间的”的 medianus 有关。这个原本与中部船帆有关的名称在不同航海传统中发生位置转移，在英语中与 mast 结合，成为主桅后方桅杆的名称。
Mizen is a variant of mizzen, which came through French from Italian mezzana and ultimately relates to Latin medianus, meaning 'middle'. A name originally associated with a middle sail shifted its reference across nautical traditions and combined with mast in English to name a mast behind the mainmast.
🧩 构词衍生:
透明复合词：mizen（后桅；后桅帆，亦作 mizzen）+ mast（桅杆）。复数为 mizenmasts；更常见的拼写是 mizzenmast，复数为 mizzenmasts；分写形式 mizen mast、mizzen mast 也可见。该词没有常用的派生动词或副词。
A transparent compound: mizen ('mizzen mast or sail', also spelled mizzen) + mast. The plural is mizenmasts; the more usual spelling is mizzenmast, plural mizzenmasts, and the spaced forms mizen mast and mizzen mast also occur. There are no usual derived verbs or adverbs.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
mizzenmast 是同一个词的常见拼写；mizzen 可在语境明确时简称后桅，也可指后桅帆；aftermast 泛指靠船尾的桅杆，准确含义取决于船型和上下文。
Mizzenmast is the usual alternative spelling of the same word; mizzen can refer to the mast when context is clear, but can also name its sail; aftermast is a broader term for a mast toward the stern, whose precise reference depends on the vessel and context.

Use the modern nautical meaning: mizen identifies the mizzen position, and mast supplies the structure—a mast behind the mainmast.

🏛️ 来源历史:
该词经盎格鲁法语 monicion 进入中古英语，源自拉丁语 monitio（提醒、告诫），其动词基础是 monere（提醒、警告）。普通的“告诫”义也发展为教会及某些法律程序中的正式传令义。
The word entered Middle English through Anglo-French monicion, from Latin monitio, meaning 'a reminding or warning', based on monere, 'to remind or warn'. The general idea of admonition also acquired a specialized meaning as a formal notice in ecclesiastical and certain other legal proceedings.
🧩 构词衍生:
可作拉丁语词干分析：monit-（提醒、告诫）+ -ion（行为或结果名词后缀）；现代英语中不是由一个常用独立词 monit 直接构成。直接相关形式有复数 monitions、形容词 monitory（告诫的）和副词 monitorily（告诫地，罕见）；admonition、premonition、premonitory、monitor 属于同源词族，并非都由 monition 派生。
A learned analysis is monit- ('remind or warn') + -ion, a suffix naming an action or result; there is no usual independent modern English base monit. Closely related forms include the plural monitions, the adjective monitory, and the rare adverb monitorily; admonition, premonition, premonitory, and monitor belong to the wider etymological family rather than all being derivatives of monition.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
warning（警告）最通用，强调危险或不良后果；admonition（告诫）常含纠正行为的意味；caution（提醒、警告）侧重谨慎行事。monition 更正式、较少见，且另有专门法律义。
Warning is the broadest term and emphasizes danger or adverse consequences; admonition often seeks to correct conduct; caution emphasizes acting carefully. Monition is more formal and uncommon, and also has a specialized legal meaning.
💡 秒记锚点:
把 monit- 与 monitor 的“提醒、监督”联系起来：monition 就是一次郑重提醒或正式告诫。
Connect monit- with the reminding or watching function of a monitor: a monition is a serious reminder or formal warning.

COBUILD: An antibody fragment or other binding molecule is monovalent if it has only one functional site for binding its target.
搭配: a monovalent antibody fragment; monovalent binding 单价抗体片段；单价结合

🏛️ 来源历史:
该词是近代科学构词，将希腊语来源的 mono-（一）与 -valent（具有某种价的）结合；后者最终来自拉丁语 valere（有力量、有能力）。它先表达单一的化学结合能力，后来也用于免疫学中的单一作用对象或单个结合位点。
The word is a modern scientific formation combining Greek-derived mono-, 'one', with -valent, 'having a specified valence', ultimately from Latin valere, 'to be strong or capable'. Its idea of a single combining capacity extended from chemistry to a single immunological target or binding site.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代构词：mono-（一）+ -valent（有……价的）。直接派生名词为 monovalence、monovalency（一价性；单价性）；相关基础词为 valence、valency（价），同系列词有 univalent（一价的）、divalent（二价的）、trivalent（三价的）、multivalent 和 polyvalent（多价的）。
Modern formation: mono- ('one') + -valent ('having a specified valence'). Direct noun derivatives are monovalence and monovalency; related base nouns are valence and valency, while parallel formations include univalent, divalent, trivalent, multivalent, and polyvalent.
⚖️ 近义微雕:

Univalent is generally equivalent in the chemical sense; single-valent is a descriptive equivalent. In immunology, monospecific means recognizing one kind of target, but does not necessarily mean having only one binding site, so it is not universally interchangeable with monovalent.
💡 秒记锚点:

Mono- means 'one' and -valent concerns combining capacity: think one valence, one target, or one binding site, depending on context.
mordent 1 词性 · 双语对齐
n. 名词 //ˈmɔːdənt// 〔音乐〕波音；主音与上方或下方邻音快速交替后返回主音的短装饰音，具体方向取决于符号、时代及术语惯例 (a short musical ornament alternating a main note with an adjacent note and returning to the main note)
COBUILD: A mordent is a short musical ornament in which you quickly move from the written note to a neighbouring note and back again, with the direction depending on the notation and convention.
搭配: an upper mordent; a lower mordent 上波音；下波音
例句: The teacher asked her to play the lower mordent clearly before continuing the melody.
老师让她先清晰地奏出这个下波音，再继续演奏旋律。
🏛️ 来源历史:
该音乐术语最终源自意大利语 mordente，字面意思是“咬的”，来自 mordere（咬），其源头是拉丁语 mordere。这个名称以短促的“咬一下”比喻主音附近快速而鲜明的装饰动作；不同历史传统对其上下方向的命名并不完全一致。
The musical term ultimately comes from Italian mordente, literally 'biting', from mordere, 'to bite', inherited from Latin mordere. Its name likens the brief, pointed ornament around a main note to a little bite; historical traditions differ in how they name its upward and downward forms.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中是整体借词，不宜硬拆为有生产力的英语词根和后缀；历史上可联系意大利语 mord-（咬）+ -ente（现在分词词尾）。复数为 mordents；常见术语组合有 upper mordent、lower mordent 和 inverted mordent，其中 inverted 的具体所指应结合所用术语体系判断。mordant（尖刻的；媒染剂）是同源词，不是 mordent 的派生词。
In modern English this is a borrowed whole rather than a productive English root-plus-suffix formation; historically it relates to Italian mord- ('bite') + -ente, a present-participle ending. Its plural is mordents; usual combinations include upper mordent, lower mordent, and inverted mordent, whose precise reference depends on the terminology used. Mordant is an etymological relative, not a derivative of mordent.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
ornament（装饰音）是上位词；trill（颤音）通常指主音与邻音持续、反复交替；turn（回音）通常围绕主音依次经过上、下邻音。这些术语相关，但不能与 mordent 任意互换。
Ornament is the broader category; trill usually involves a more sustained repetition between a main note and its neighbour; turn usually moves around the main note through both upper and lower neighbours. These are related terms rather than freely interchangeable synonyms.
💡 秒记锚点:
mord-＝咬：旋律在邻音上轻快“咬一下”，马上回到主音。
Mord- means 'bite': the melody takes a quick bite at a neighbouring note, then returns to the main note.

n. 名词 //ˌmɪθəʊˈmeɪniə// 说谎癖；反复编造或夸大事实的强烈倾向 (a compulsive tendency to invent stories or exaggerate the truth)
COBUILD: Mythomania is a strong, compulsive tendency to tell lies or invent exaggerated stories about yourself and your experiences.
搭配: suffer from mythomania; a case of mythomania 有说谎癖；一例说谎癖
例句: The narrator's mythomania makes his accounts of childhood difficult to trust.
叙述者有说谎癖，因此他对童年的讲述很难令人信服。
🏛️ 来源历史:
英语借自法语 mythomanie，由源于希腊语 mythos（故事、传说）和 mania（狂热、疯狂）的成分构成。它把“编造故事”与难以控制的倾向联系起来，成为描述病态说谎的术语。
English borrowed mythomania from French mythomanie, formed from elements derived from Greek mythos, meaning 'story' or 'legend,' and mania, meaning 'madness' or 'frenzy.' The combination became a term for a compulsive tendency to fabricate stories.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代构词：mytho-（故事、神话）＋-mania（狂热或强迫性倾向）。主要词族：mythomaniac（名词：有说谎癖的人；形容词：有说谎癖的）、mythomaniacal（形容词：说谎成癖的）；这些是词族形式，不宜机械地按完整词直接加后缀理解。
Modern formation: mytho- ('story, myth') + -mania ('an excessive or compulsive inclination'). Principal family forms include mythomaniac, a noun meaning a person with this tendency and an adjective describing it, and mythomaniacal, an adjective meaning compulsively untruthful; these involve changes to the ending rather than simple suffix addition to the full word.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
pathological lying（病态说谎）是最接近的表达，强调反复而失常的说谎行为；compulsive lying（强迫性说谎）强调难以控制的冲动；mendacity（虚假、惯于说谎）侧重不诚实本身，不必涉及强迫性。
Pathological lying is the closest expression and emphasizes a persistent, abnormal pattern; compulsive lying highlights the difficulty of resisting the urge; mendacity means untruthfulness without necessarily implying compulsion.
💡 秒记锚点:
mytho-＝编故事，-mania＝停不下来：编故事成了难以控制的习惯。
Story-making plus compulsion: inventing stories becomes a habit that is hard to control.

In modern English, nepenthe is normally treated as a whole borrowed word rather than a combination of productive English parts. Its historical elements are Greek nē- ('without') and penthos ('grief'); the principal derivative is nepenthean, formed with -an and meaning sorrow-banishing.

Solace emphasizes comfort in sadness; anodyne emphasizes something that eases physical or emotional pain; oblivion is the state of forgetting or unconsciousness, whereas nepenthe is the imagined substance or comforting thing that produces relief.

历史词根是“没有悲伤”：喝下文学中的忘忧饮，忧愁暂时退去。
Its historical logic is 'without grief': imagine a literary drink that lets sorrow fade.

v. 不及物动词 //ˈnɪkteɪt// 〔罕用、专业〕眨眼 (to blink or wink)
COBUILD: When a person or animal nictates, they briefly close and open an eyelid.
搭配: nictate rapidly; nictate reflexively 快速眨眼；反射性地眨眼

强光亮起时，那只动物开始快速眨眼。
🏛️ 来源历史:

Nictate comes from nictat-, the participial stem of Latin nictare, meaning 'to blink' or 'to wink.' English preserved the Latin stem in a learned verb, while ordinary speech generally uses blink or wink.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代学习可分析为 nict-（与眨眼有关的拉丁来源词干）＋-ate（动词结尾），但它整体来自拉丁语词干。屈折形式：nictates、nictated、nictating；相关词族：nictation（眨眼）、nictitate（眨眼）、nictitation（眨眼）、nictitating（用于 nictitating membrane，意为“瞬膜”）。
For modern learning, the word can be analyzed as nict- (a Latin-derived stem associated with blinking) + the verbal ending -ate, although it was borrowed from a Latin stem. Inflections are nictates, nictated, and nictating; related forms include nictation, nictitate, nictitation, and nictitating, as in nictitating membrane, the movable third eyelid.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
blink（眨眼）是最普通的词，常指快速闭合并睁开双眼；wink（眨一只眼）常带有示意或暗示；nictitate 与 nictate 基本同义，同样属于专业或罕用表达。
Blink is the ordinary word, often describing the quick closing and reopening of both eyes; wink commonly means closing one eye as a signal; nictitate is essentially synonymous with nictate and is likewise technical or uncommon.
💡 秒记锚点:
把 nict- 与“瞬膜”的 nictitating 联系起来：共同的核心动作都是眨眼。
Connect nict- with nictitating membrane: the shared idea is an eyelid moving in a blink.
nonage 2 词性 · 双语对齐
n. 名词 //ˈnɒnɪdʒ// 〔旧式、法律用语〕未成年时期；未成年状态 (the period or condition of being below the legal age of adulthood)
COBUILD: A person's nonage is the period when they have not yet reached the legal age of adulthood.
搭配: during someone's nonage; a period of nonage 在某人未成年期间；一段未成年时期
例句: During his nonage, his property was managed by a guardian.
在他未成年期间，他的财产由监护人管理。
n. 名词 //ˈnɒnɪdʒ// 〔文学，比喻〕初创期；尚未成熟的阶段 (an early or immature stage of development)
COBUILD: The nonage of something is an early stage in its development, before it has become mature or fully established.
搭配: in its nonage; the nonage of a movement 在其尚未成熟时；一场运动的初创期
例句: The essay describes the difficulties the movement faced in its nonage.
这篇文章描述了这场运动在初创期面临的困难。
🏛️ 来源历史:
经盎格鲁法语进入中古英语，由表示否定的 non- 和 age（年龄）构成，原指尚未达到法定成年年龄的状态。后来，这种“尚未成年”的含义被比喻性地用于事物尚未成熟的阶段。
Nonage entered Middle English through Anglo-French, combining negative non- with age and referring to the condition of not having reached legal adulthood. The idea of being underage later extended figuratively to an immature stage of development.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代构词：non-（未、不）＋age（此处指成年年龄）；这里的 age 是独立名词，不是名词后缀 -age。直接词族较小，有罕用形容词 nonaged（未成年的）；相关 age 词族包括 aged（年老的）、ageing/aging（衰老；正在变老的）、ageless（不显老的；永恒的）、underage（未成年的）和 overage（超龄的）。
Modern structure: non- ('not') + age, here referring to legal adulthood; age is the independent noun, not the noun-forming suffix -age. The direct family is small and includes the rare adjective nonaged; related members of the broader age family include aged, ageing/aging, ageless, underage, and overage.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
minority（未成年状态）是较正式的法律对应词；childhood（童年）按人生阶段描述，通常不涵盖整个未成年时期；immaturity（不成熟）强调发展程度，可以用于任何年龄的人或尚未成熟的事物。
Minority is the formal legal equivalent for the condition of being underage; childhood describes a life stage and usually covers less than the whole period before adulthood; immaturity concerns incomplete development and can apply at any age or to developing things.
💡 秒记锚点:
non-＋age：还没到“成年年龄”；引申为事物还没长成熟。
Non- plus age: not yet of age, and figuratively not yet fully developed.
octant 3 词性 · 双语对齐
n. 名词 //ˈɒktənt// 八分仪；以八分之一圆弧为刻度弧的反射式测角仪 (a reflecting instrument with a one-eighth-circle arc, used especially in navigation)
COBUILD: An octant is an instrument with an arc of forty-five degrees that navigators used to measure angles, especially the height of the sun or a star above the horizon.
搭配: a reflecting octant; use an octant 反射式八分仪；使用八分仪
例句: The navigator used an octant to measure the sun's altitude above the horizon.
领航员用八分仪测量太阳在地平线以上的高度角。
n. 名词 //ˈɒktənt// 〔几何〕圆周的八分之一；四十五度圆弧或扇形 (one eighth of a circle, corresponding to an angle of forty-five degrees)
COBUILD: An octant is one of eight equal parts of a circle, with an angle of forty-five degrees at the centre.
搭配: an octant of a circle; divide a circle into octants 圆的八分之一；把圆八等分
例句: Each octant of the circle subtends an angle of forty-five degrees at the centre.
圆的每个八分之一部分所对的圆心角都是四十五度。
n. 名词 //ˈɒktənt// 〔数学〕卦限；三个坐标平面划分三维空间所得的八个区域之一 (one of the eight regions into which the coordinate planes divide three-dimensional space)
COBUILD: An octant is one of the eight regions formed when the three coordinate planes divide three-dimensional space.
搭配: the first octant; the positive octant 第一卦限；正卦限
例句: The point lies in the first octant because all three coordinates are positive.
这个点位于第一卦限，因为它的三个坐标都为正。
🏛️ 来源历史:
来自拉丁语 octans，属格为 octantis，意为“八分之一”，与 octo（八）有关。仪器因刻度弧占整圆的八分之一而得名；同一“八等分”的概念也用于几何和三维坐标空间。
Octant comes from Latin octans, with the stem octant-, meaning 'an eighth' and related to octo, 'eight.' The instrument was named for its one-eighth-circle graduated arc, while the same division into eight underlies the geometric and spatial meanings.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中通常把 octant 作为完整词处理；oct- 提示“八”，但结尾 -ant 在这里不是表示人的后缀。词族：octants（复数）、octantal（形容词：八分之一圆的、卦限的）；同源相关词包括 octal（八进制的）、octagon（八边形）和 octave（八度），它们不是由 octant 直接派生的。
Modern English normally treats octant as a whole word; oct- signals 'eight,' but -ant here is not an agent suffix. Family forms include the plural octants and the adjective octantal, meaning relating to an octant; related words sharing the historical 'eight' element include octal, octagon, and octave, which are not direct derivatives of octant.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
sector（扇形）是圆的一部分，角度不限定为四十五度；quadrant（象限；四分之一圆）涉及四等分，而 octant 涉及八等分；sextant（六分仪）是相关航海仪器，刻度弧为整圆的六分之一。
Sector is a general circular region with no fixed angle; quadrant concerns division into four, whereas octant concerns division into eight; sextant names a related navigational instrument whose graduated arc is one sixth of a circle.
💡 秒记锚点:
抓住 oct-＝八：圆分八份，每份四十五度；空间被三个坐标平面分成八个卦限。
Anchor oct- to eight: eight equal circular parts give forty-five degrees each, and three coordinate planes create eight spatial regions.
