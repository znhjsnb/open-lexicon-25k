durst 1 词性 · 双语对齐
v. 动词（古旧过去式） //dɜːst// 敢于；胆敢（dare 的古旧过去式） (Dared; had the courage or boldness to do something, in archaic usage.)
COBUILD: If someone durst do something, they had the courage or boldness to do it; this form is now archaic.
搭配: durst not speak; durst not ask 不敢说话；不敢询问
例句: None durst question the king's command.
无人敢质疑国王的命令。
🏛️ 来源历史:
durst 源自中古英语 durste，承自古英语 dorste，是 dare 所属历史动词的过去式。现代英语通常使用 dared，durst 主要保留在古旧文学和仿古表达中。
Durst comes from Middle English durste and Old English dorste, the past-tense form of the historical verb underlying dare. Modern English normally uses dared, while durst survives chiefly in older literature and deliberately archaic language.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代分析：durst 是 dare 的历史不规则过去式，不能拆成现代词根和词缀，也不添加 -ed。主要词族：dare（敢；挑战）、dares（第三人称单数；挑战的复数）、dared（现代过去式及过去分词）、daring（现在分词；大胆的；胆量）、daringly（大胆地）、daringness（大胆，较少用）。
Modern analysis: durst is a historical irregular past tense of dare, with no productive root-and-affix breakdown and no added -ed. The principal family comprises dare, dares, dared, daring as a participle, adjective, and noun, daringly, and the less common noun daringness.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
dared 是现代英语中最直接的对应形式；ventured 强调冒着风险或犹豫着尝试，常接 to do。durst 本身带有鲜明的古旧文体色彩。
Dared is the direct modern equivalent; ventured emphasizes attempting something despite risk or hesitation and commonly takes a to-infinitive. Durst carries a distinctly archaic literary tone.
💡 秒记锚点:
把 durst 当作 dare 的“古装过去式”：durst not＝过去不敢。
Think of durst as dare in historical costume: durst not means did not dare.
emic 1 词性 · 双语对齐
adj. 形容词 //ˈiːmɪk// 依据文化内部成员自身的概念、分类和意义体系的 (Based on the concepts, categories, and meanings used within a particular culture.)
COBUILD: An emic account explains a culture or practice through the distinctions and meanings recognized by the people who belong to it.
搭配: an emic perspective; an emic approach 文化内部视角；基于内部意义体系的研究方法
例句: The researcher adopted an emic perspective to understand what the ceremony meant to local participants.
研究者采用文化内部视角，理解这一仪式对当地参与者的意义。
🏛️ 来源历史:
语言学家 Kenneth L. Pike 在20世纪中叶从 phonemic（音位的）截取并创用了 emic，与从 phonetic 截取的 etic 配对。这个术语随后用于人类学等领域，表示按照某一文化内部有意义的区别来理解它。
Linguist Kenneth L. Pike coined emic in the mid-twentieth century by shortening phonemic, pairing it with etic from phonetic. The term subsequently spread to anthropology and related fields to describe analysis using distinctions meaningful within a culture.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代分析：emic 是由 phonemic 截短形成的独立词，通常作为整体词基处理；emic + -ally → emically（从文化内部视角地）。相关词 emics 指对内部分类和意义体系的研究；etic、etically 是与其配对的对照术语。
Modern analysis: emic is a clipping of phonemic and normally functions as an independent base; emic + -ally produces emically. The related noun emics concerns the study of internal categories and meanings; etic and etically are its contrasting companion terms.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
insider-oriented 强调面向内部成员的视角；participant-centered 强调以参与者的经验为中心。emic 更严格地强调采用文化内部成员自己的分类和意义体系，这些表达只是近义释语。
Insider-oriented emphasizes an internal viewpoint, while participant-centered emphasizes participants' experiences. Emic more specifically requires attention to the culture's own categories and meanings, so these expressions are approximate equivalents.
💡 秒记锚点:
phonemic 关注语言内部有意义的区别；emic 关注文化内部有意义的区别。
Phonemic tracks meaningful distinctions within a language; emic tracks meaningful distinctions within a culture.

Epizootic derives from French épizootique, modeled on épidémique with an element from Greek zōion, 'animal,' replacing the element referring to people. English also developed a noun use for an outbreak of disease among animals.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代科技构词可分析为 epi-（在……之上、遍及）+ zoot-（动物）+ -ic（形容词后缀），词干与词缀结合形成 epizootic；同形名词表示一次流行。主要词族包括 epizootics（名词复数）、epizootically（以动物疫病流行的方式）、epizootiology 或 epizootology（动物疫病流行学）、epizootiological 或 epizootological（动物疫病流行学的）、epizootiologist 或 epizootologist（动物疫病流行学家）。
In scientific word formation, epizootic can be analyzed as epi- 'upon or throughout' + zoot- 'animal' + adjectival -ic; the same form also functions as a noun. Principal family members include epizootics, epizootically, epizootiology or epizootology, epizootiological or epizootological, and epizootiologist or epizootologist.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
animal epidemic 是清楚直接的近义表达；outbreak 泛指一次疾病暴发，并不限定动物或流行规模；epidemic 通常用于人群，也可用于更广泛的流行病学语境。epizootic 明确限定动物群体。
Animal epidemic is a close plain-language equivalent; outbreak denotes an episode of disease without specifying animals or its scale. Epidemic usually concerns people but also appears in broader epidemiological usage, whereas epizootic explicitly concerns animal populations.
💡 秒记锚点:

COBUILD: An excimer is a short-lived complex of two atoms or molecules of the same kind that bind together in an excited electronic state but do not form a stable bound pair in the corresponding ground state.

🏛️ 来源历史:

Excimer is a twentieth-century scientific blend of excited and dimer, naming a pair whose binding depends on electronic excitation. In laser technology, excimer laser is also widely used for certain devices whose active complexes are, strictly speaking, excited pairs of unlike species.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代构词：exci(ted) + (di)mer → excimer，是拼合词，不能分析成 ex- 加 cimer。主要词形和相关表达：excimers（复数）、excimer formation（激发态二聚体形成）、excimer emission（激发态二聚体发射）、excimer laser（准分子激光器）；没有常用的独立形容词或动词派生形式，exciplex 是相关的对照术语。
Modern formation: exci(ted) + (di)mer → excimer, a blend rather than ex- plus cimer. Principal forms and expressions include the plural excimers, excimer formation, excimer emission, and excimer laser; there are no widely used separate adjective or verb derivatives, and exciplex is a related contrasting term.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
excited-state dimer 是最直接的释义性近义表达；excited-state complex 是更宽泛的上位表达。exciplex 是相关术语而非严格同义词，通常指由不同种类粒子形成的激发态复合体。
Excited-state dimer is the closest explanatory equivalent, while excited-state complex is a broader category. Exciplex is a related term rather than an exact synonym and generally refers to an excited complex formed from unlike species.
💡 秒记锚点:

Excited + dimer: the pair binds when excited and loses that stable binding in the ground state.

n. 名词 //ɡroʊˈtɛskəri// 怪诞的人、事物或形象 (A bizarre, distorted, or absurd person, thing, or representation.)
COBUILD: A grotesquerie is something whose strange or exaggerated form makes it seem disturbing, absurd, or comical.
搭配: architectural grotesqueries; a collection of grotesqueries 怪诞的建筑形象；一组怪诞事物
例句: The walls were covered with painted grotesqueries.
墙上满是描绘怪诞形象的画作。
🏛️ 来源历史:
grotesquerie 由 grotesque 加法语式名词后缀 -erie 构成。grotesque 最终源自意大利语 grottesca，原指在被视作“洞穴”的古罗马遗址中发现的奇异装饰，后来泛指怪诞、扭曲或荒谬的事物。
Grotesquerie was formed from grotesque with the French-style noun suffix -erie. Grotesque ultimately comes from Italian grottesca, originally referring to fanciful decoration discovered in ancient Roman ruins resembling caves, before broadening to the bizarre, distorted, or absurd.
🧩 构词衍生:
构词公式为 grotesque（怪诞的）+ -erie（法语式名词后缀，对应 -ery）；表示具体事物时复数为 grotesqueries。核心词族包括 grotesque（形容词：怪诞的；名词：怪诞形象）、grotesquely（怪诞地）和 grotesqueness（怪诞性）。
The structure is grotesque + -erie, a French-style noun suffix corresponding to -ery; the countable plural is grotesqueries. The core family includes grotesque as an adjective and noun, grotesquely, and grotesqueness.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
grotesqueness（怪诞性）最接近其抽象义，直接强调性质；bizarreness（离奇）强调不同寻常，不一定涉及形体扭曲；absurdity（荒谬或荒谬之事）强调不合逻辑。grotesquerie 尤其容易让人联想到夸张变形的艺术形象。
Grotesqueness is closest to the abstract sense and directly names the quality; bizarreness emphasizes unusualness without necessarily implying distortion; absurdity emphasizes illogicality. Grotesquerie particularly suggests exaggerated or distorted artistic forms.
💡 秒记锚点:
grotesque 是“怪诞的”，-erie 把它变成“怪诞感”或“怪诞之物”。
Grotesque describes something bizarre; -erie turns that description into the quality or the bizarre thing itself.
hardbound 1 词性 · 双语对齐
CEFR-C1
adj. 形容词 //ˈhɑːrdbaʊnd// 硬封面装订的；精装的 (Bound in a stiff, durable cover.)
COBUILD: A hardbound book has a stiff cover that protects its pages.
搭配: hardbound book; hardbound edition 精装书；精装版
例句: The library ordered a hardbound edition of the dictionary.
图书馆订购了这部词典的精装版。
🏛️ 来源历史:
hardbound 由 hard（硬的）和 bound（bind 的过去分词，此处意为“装订的”）复合而成。它把装订工艺直接转化为书籍形式的名称，用来表示采用硬封面的装订方式。
Hardbound combines hard with bound, the past participle of bind in its bookbinding sense. It directly describes a method of binding books with stiff covers.
🧩 构词衍生:
构词公式为 hard（硬的）+ bound（装订的），属于复合形容词；bound 来自不规则动词 bind—bound—bound。相关词族有 bind（装订、捆绑）、binding（装订、书皮）、binder（装订工、活页夹）和 bookbinding（书籍装订）；hardback、hardcover 是同义复合词，softbound 是对应反义词。
The structure is hard + bound, forming a compound adjective; bound belongs to the irregular verb pattern bind–bound–bound. Related words include bind, binding, binder, and bookbinding; hardback and hardcover are synonymous compounds, while softbound is the corresponding antonym.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
hardcover 和 hardback 在描述书籍装帧时都与 hardbound 基本同义，且也常作名词。clothbound（布面精装的）进一步限定封面包覆材料是布，而 hardbound 只说明封面坚硬。
Hardcover and hardback are essentially synonymous with hardbound when describing book formats, and both also commonly function as nouns. Clothbound additionally specifies a cloth covering, whereas hardbound specifies only that the cover is stiff.
💡 秒记锚点:
hard 是“硬”，bound 是“装订好”：装进硬封面，就是精装。
Hard means stiff; bound means bound into a book: stiff covers make a hardbound book.
