Innervate was formed as a medical term from Latin nervus, meaning “nerve” or “sinew,” with in-, meaning “into,” and the verb-forming suffix -ate. Innervated is its past tense and past participle and also describes the state of tissue supplied with nerves.
🧩 构词衍生:

⚖️ 近义微雕:

alacrity 1 词性 · 双语对齐
GRECEFR-C2
n. 名词 //əˈlækrəti// 欣然应允或行动的爽快劲头；积极敏捷 (Cheerful eagerness and readiness to act promptly.)
COBUILD: If you do something with alacrity, you do it quickly and willingly because you are eager to do it.
搭配: with alacrity; accept with alacrity 欣然且迅速地；欣然接受
例句: She accepted the invitation with alacrity.
她欣然接受了邀请，答应得十分爽快。
🏛️ 来源历史:
alacrity 来自拉丁语 alacritas（活泼、热切），其基础是形容词 alacer（欢快的、活跃的）。英语中的词义逐渐集中于一种带着愉快和热情、随时愿意迅速行动的状态。
Alacrity comes from Latin alacritas, meaning 'liveliness' or 'eagerness', based on alacer, meaning 'cheerful' or 'lively'. In English, its meaning centres on cheerful willingness combined with prompt action.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中通常把 alacrity 作为完整词基学习；历史上可识别出拉丁语词干 alacr- 与抽象名词成分，对应英语形式 -ity，但不存在常用的独立英语词 alacr。相关词有罕见形容词 alacritous（欣然敏捷的）及其副词 alacritously（欣然敏捷地）；日常更常用 with alacrity，没有常用的对应动词。
In modern English, alacrity is usually learned as a complete lexical base. Historically, it contains the Latin stem alacr- and an abstract-noun ending corresponding to English -ity, but alacr is not a common independent English word. Related forms include the rare adjective alacritous and its adverb alacritously; with alacrity is much more usual, and there is no common corresponding verb.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
eagerness（热切）突出强烈意愿；readiness（乐意；准备就绪）突出愿意或能够立即行动；promptness（迅速及时）强调行动不拖延。alacrity 把热情、乐意和迅速结合起来，常带有愉快爽快的意味。
Eagerness emphasizes strong desire; readiness emphasizes willingness or preparedness to act; promptness emphasizes acting without delay. Alacrity combines enthusiasm, willingness, and speed, usually with a cheerful quality.
💡 秒记锚点:
不是单纯“快”，而是“乐意得马上行动”：欣然＋迅速。
Think 'so willing that you act at once': cheerful eagerness plus speed.
garret 1 词性 · 双语对齐
GRECEFR-C2
n. 名词 //ˈɡærət// （通常狭小简陋的）屋顶下的小房间；阁楼房间 (A small, often poorly furnished room directly beneath a roof.)
COBUILD: A garret is a small room at the top of a house, just under the roof, often thought of as a poor person's living space.
搭配: a tiny garret; live in a garret 狭小的阁楼房间；住在阁楼小屋里
例句: The young writer rented a tiny garret above a bakery.
那位年轻作家租住在一家面包店楼上的狭小阁楼里。
🏛️ 来源历史:
garret 经中古英语来自古法语 garite，原指瞭望塔或岗亭，与表示守护、保护的动词有关。词义后来转向房屋最上部的空间，最终常指紧贴屋顶、可供居住的小房间。
Garret entered Middle English from Old French garite, meaning a watchtower or lookout shelter, and is related to a verb meaning 'to guard or protect'. Its meaning shifted to the uppermost part of a house and then especially to a small room immediately beneath the roof.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中 garret 是完整词基，没有可用于学习的常用词根、词缀拆分；复数为 garrets。罕见相关词包括 garret + -ed → garreted（有阁楼的）和 garret + -eer → garreteer（住阁楼的穷文人）；常见组合有 garret room（阁楼房间），没有常用的派生动词或副词。
In modern English, garret is a complete base without a useful productive root-and-affix breakdown; its plural is garrets. Rare related forms include garret + -ed → garreted, meaning 'having a garret', and garret + -eer → garreteer, a poor writer living in a garret. Garret room is a familiar combination; there is no common derived verb or adverb.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
attic（阁楼）泛指屋顶下的空间，可用于储物或居住；loft（阁楼、顶层空间）可指较开敞的屋顶空间或改建住宅；room（房间）不限定位置。garret 更具体地指屋顶下的小房间，常带有贫寒、简陋或文人清苦生活的联想。
Attic broadly names the space beneath a roof, whether used for storage or living; loft can refer to an open upper space or a converted dwelling; room does not specify location. Garret more specifically suggests a small room beneath the roof, often associated with poverty, sparse furnishings, or a struggling writer's life.
💡 秒记锚点:
从高处的瞭望岗亭，记到屋顶下的小房间。
Picture a lookout shelter high above the ground, then a small room tucked beneath a roof.
