🏛️ 来源历史:
blur 的词源尚不确定，早期用法涉及涂污、污点和模糊不清，可能与 blear（使视力模糊）有关。其意义后来从视觉上的不清楚扩展到界限、区别和记忆的不清晰。
The origin of blur is uncertain; early uses concerned smearing, marks, and indistinctness, and a connection with blear has been suggested. Its meaning later extended from visual indistinctness to unclear boundaries, distinctions, and memories.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代英语中 blur 是不可再作常用词素拆分的基本词，名词与动词同形。屈折变化：blurs、blurred、blurring；加 -ed 或 -ing 时双写 r。常用派生：blur + -y → blurry（模糊的），blurry + -ness → blurriness（模糊）；blurred 也常作形容词。英式英语 /blɜː/，美式英语 /blɝː/。
In modern English, blur is a simple base with identical noun and verb forms. Inflections are blurs, blurred, and blurring, with r doubled before -ed and -ing. Common derivatives are blur + -y → blurry and blurry + -ness → blurriness; blurred also functions as an adjective. British pronunciation is /blɜː/ and American pronunciation is /blɝː/.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
obscure（遮蔽；使难以理解）强调妨碍看见或理解；smudge（抹花；污痕）强调擦抹造成的痕迹；haze（薄雾；朦胧）强调雾状遮罩。blur 的核心是轮廓、细节或区别失去清晰度。
Obscure emphasizes preventing sight or understanding; smudge emphasizes a mark caused by rubbing or smearing; haze suggests a mist-like veil. Blur centers on the loss of clear outlines, details, or distinctions.
💡 秒记锚点:

Picture edges dissolving: dissolved image edges are hard to see, and dissolved conceptual boundaries are hard to distinguish.

🏛️ 来源历史:
动词 poke 在中古英语时期已有记录，确切来源不明，可能与荷兰语或低地德语中表示戳、推的词有关。“伸出”“翻找”和网络上的“戳一下”都可理解为对短促推动动作的延伸。
The verb poke is recorded in Middle English, but its exact origin is uncertain; it may be related to Dutch or Low German words for pushing or prodding. Its uses for protruding, searching, and sending an online poke extend the idea of a brief physical push.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代 poke 是基本词，名词与动词同形：pokes、poked、poking；加 -ing 时去掉词尾不发音的 e。常用派生有 poke + -er → poker（拨火棍），以及 poky／pokey（动作慢的；其“狭小的”义不宜机械套用同一分析）、pokily（缓慢地）、pokiness（缓慢）。纸牌游戏 poker 与“拨火棍”不是同一词源；a pig in a poke 中表示“袋子”的 poke 也是另一个词。
Modern poke is a simple base used as both noun and verb: pokes, poked, and poking; the silent final e is dropped before -ing. Related forms include poke + -er → poker, meaning a fire-tending tool, and poky/pokey, pokily, and pokiness in the sense of slowness; the 'cramped' sense of poky should not automatically receive the same analysis. Poker the card game has a separate origin, as does poke meaning 'bag' in a pig in a poke.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
prod（戳；催促）常带有促使对方行动的目的；jab（猛戳；猛刺）强调动作快而有力；nudge（轻推）通常更轻柔，常用于提醒或暗示。poke 强调用手指或尖物接触、顶入。
Prod often suggests trying to make someone act; jab emphasizes a quick, forceful thrust; nudge is usually gentler and often signals a reminder or suggestion. Poke emphasizes contact or penetration with a finger or pointed object.
💡 秒记锚点:
从“一指戳过去”展开：戳到人是碰触，戳出边缘是露出，四处戳探是翻找。
Start with one finger pushing forward: touching someone gives a poke, pushing beyond an edge means protruding, and probing here and there becomes searching.

🏛️ 来源历史:
表示尖钉的 spike 通常追溯至古诺斯语 spík（尖钉），后来发展出刺穿、扣球和图表中尖峰式上涨等意义。表示谷穗或穗状花序的 spike 原本是另一个词，来自拉丁语 spica（穗），不能把两者简单归为同一词源。
Spike meaning a pointed nail is generally traced to Old Norse spík and later developed senses involving piercing, forceful ball strikes, and sharp graphical peaks. Spike meaning an ear of grain or flower cluster is historically a separate word from Latin spica, meaning 'ear of grain.'
🧩 构词衍生:

Modern spike is a simple noun and verb base: spikes, spiked, and spiking, with final e dropped before -ing. Derivatives include spike + -y → spiky, also spelled spikey, plus spikily, spikiness, spiker, and spike + -let → spikelet. Spiked also functions as an adjective meaning fitted with points or containing added alcohol or drugs. Spikelet belongs to the botanical branch of the word.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
point（尖端）泛指尖头，spike 通常指突出的硬质尖刺；surge（激增）强调强劲增长，spike 更强调陡峭、常较短暂的峰值；lace（往饮食中掺入）常指加入酒或其他物质，spike 在饮料语境中也可特别指秘密下药。
Point is a general term for a sharp tip, whereas a spike is usually a projecting rigid point. Surge emphasizes a strong increase, while spike highlights a steep, often brief peak. Lace describes adding a substance to food or drink; spike can specifically suggest secretly adding a drug to a drink.
💡 秒记锚点:
抓住“尖头”：实物是尖刺，曲线向上冒尖是骤增，头发立成尖束也是 spike；植物学的“穗”另有词源。
Remember a sharp point: a physical point is a spike, a graph shooting into a point shows a spike, and pointed upright hair is spiked. The botanical 'ear' sense has a separate origin.

🏛️ 来源历史:
grease 经古法语 graisse／gresse 进入英语，最终与拉丁语 crassus（肥的；厚的）有关。它从动物脂肪发展出润滑脂等意义，而动词义则来自给物体涂上这种物质的动作。
Grease entered English through Old French graisse/gresse and is ultimately connected with Latin crassus, meaning 'fat' or 'thick.' Its meaning extended from animal fat to lubricating substances, while the verb developed from applying such material.
🧩 构词衍生:
现代 grease 是基本词，兼作名词和动词；变化为 greases、greased、greasing，加 -ing 时去 e。常用派生：grease + -y → greasy（油腻的），greasily（油腻地），greasiness（油腻）；greaser（加油工；涂油装置等），greaseless（无油脂的），de- + grease → degrease（除油），degreaser（除油剂），degreasing（除油）。greasy 的 s 也有 /s/、/z/ 两种读法；名词 grease 通常读 /ɡriːs/，动词两种读法均可。
Modern grease is a simple noun and verb base, with the forms greases, greased, and greasing; final e is dropped before -ing. Common derivatives are grease + -y → greasy, plus greasily, greasiness, greaser, greaseless, and de- + grease → degrease, with degreaser and degreasing. Greasy also allows /s/ and /z/ pronunciations; the noun grease normally has /ɡriːs/, while the verb allows both /ɡriːs/ and /ɡriːz/.
⚖️ 近义微雕:
fat（脂肪；油脂）范围更广，可指人体脂肪和食用脂肪；oil（油）通常强调液态；lubricant（润滑剂）强调减少摩擦的功能，并不一定是油脂。grease 通常指较稠的油脂、润滑脂或油污。
Fat is broader and can refer to body fat or cooking fat. Oil usually denotes a liquid. Lubricant emphasizes the function of reducing friction and need not be fatty. Grease typically suggests thick fat, thick lubricant, or oily residue.
💡 秒记锚点:
记住“一层滑油”：厨房里是油脂，机器上是润滑脂，涂上这一层就是动词 grease。
Picture a slippery coating: in the kitchen it is cooking grease, on machinery it is lubricant, and applying the coating is to grease.

n. 名词 /英 /ˈmɔɪstʃə/；美 /ˈmɔɪstʃɚ// 水分；湿气；表面的细小水滴 (water present in small amounts in the air, on a surface, or within a substance)

Moisture comes from Old French moistour, meaning dampness or wetness, related to moiste, meaning moist. In English, it came to refer chiefly to water present in the air, within materials, or on surfaces.
🧩 构词衍生:
形态联系可表示为 moist（微湿的）+ -ure（名词后缀）→ moisture（水分），但历史上全词是从法语借入的。常见词族：moist（微湿的）、moistness（微湿状态）、moisten（使湿润）；moisture 去末尾 e 加 -ize/-ise → moisturize/moisturise（给皮肤补水），进一步有 moisturizer/moisturiser（保湿霜；保湿剂）、moisturizing/moisturising（保湿的）和 moisturization/moisturisation（保湿过程）。Moisture 通常是不可数名词。
The morphological relationship can be represented as moist + the noun suffix -ure → moisture, although the whole word was historically borrowed from French. Common family members include moist, moistness, and moisten. Removing the final e of moisture and adding -ize/-ise gives moisturize/moisturise, followed by moisturizer/moisturiser, moisturizing/moisturising, and moisturization/moisturisation. Moisture is normally uncountable.
⚖️ 近义微雕:

Dampness emphasizes a slightly wet condition, often with an unpleasant association. Humidity specifically concerns water vapor in the air. Wetness emphasizes the state or degree of being wet, often suggesting more noticeable water than moisture does.
💡 秒记锚点:

Cargo is a simple borrowed word with no independently meaningful modern root-and-affix division. Freight emphasizes commercial transport and can also mean the transport service or its charge. Shipment emphasizes a particular batch of goods sent together or the act of sending it. Load emphasizes the quantity or weight carried by a vehicle.

In modern English, civic is usually treated as a complete adjective base. Historically, it contains civ-, relating to citizens, and -ic, meaning relating to; civ is not an independent English word. Family members include civics, a subject name normally treated as singular, civically, civic-minded, and civic-mindedness. Civil often concerns rights, law, or nonmilitary matters, whereas civic emphasizes citizenship, participation, and a community's public life.
